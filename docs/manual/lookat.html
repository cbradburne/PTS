Look-at tracking

The signature move — the camera keeps aiming at a fixed subject while the slider travels past.

Normally pan, tilt, slider and zoom are independent. In look-at mode, pan and tilt stop being things you set directly and become derived: the mount computes them continuously from the slider's position so the shot stays locked on a subject while the camera orbits past on the rail. Calibrate a subject once and the rig keeps it framed for you.

Before you can use it

Look-at has a few one-time prerequisites. Set these up per camera in Configuration:

  1. Has slider is enabled and the slider is homed — look-at is a slider move, so it needs one.
  2. Find Limits has been run, so the mount knows the rail's full millimetre range.
  3. Look-at mode is enabled on that camera's tab.

Calibrating a subject

You teach the mount where a subject is in 3D by aiming at it from two points on the rail. Two sight-lines cross in space, and the mount solves for the subject's position by triangulation. Each mount holds up to eight named subjects, stored in its memory.

  1. Start Add Subject (PC app, console or web). The slider moves itself to the home end of the rail.
  2. Jog pan/tilt until the camera is aimed dead-on the subject, then press Set.
  3. The slider moves to the far end. Re-aim at the same subject and press Set again.
  4. The mount solves the subject's 3D position and saves it. Give it a name.
Aim carefully The two aims are the whole calibration — the more precisely you put the subject in frame at both ends, the tighter the tracking. Pick a clear feature to aim at (a presenter's eyeline, a mark on the set).

Set Ref — once per session

Because pan and tilt have no end stops, the mount loses its absolute angle reference every time it powers off — or if someone nudges the head by hand. Look-at needs that reference, so it's re-established with Set Ref:

  1. Aim the camera at any already-calibrated subject.
  2. Press Ref (Config → Homing & Reference) and it zeroes the angular reference at the current position.

No motion is commanded — Ref only updates the mount's internal angle offset. Press it at the start of a session, after repositioning the head, or any time you suspect tracking has drifted. If the first try looks slightly off, re-aim and press Ref again.

Look-at is gated on Set Ref If you start a look-at move before setting the reference this session, the mount rejects it and every controller shows a warning. Set Ref first.

Running a move

With a subject selected, a look-at move sends the slider to one end of the rail while pan/tilt keep the subject framed the whole way:

  • ◀ / ▶ one-tap moves send the slider to the min or max end. A live arrow shows the direction on every screen at once — whoever started it.
  • Pre-aim: pan and tilt settle onto the subject before the slider starts, so the move begins already composed.
  • Switch subjects mid-move from any controller — the shot re-aims smoothly to the new subject while the slider keeps travelling.

Look-at vs. CV tracking Look-at follows a calibrated point in space — precise, repeatable, and it needs no video feed. CV tracking follows what the camera actually sees. Look-at is ideal when the subject holds a spot (a lectern, a set mark); CV is better for a subject that wanders.
