How it fits together

The mental model behind the whole system — worth five minutes before you dive into any one part.

PTS has one idea at its core: the mounts do the work and remember everything; the hub is a switchboard; and every controller is just an interchangeable window onto the same rig. Once that clicks, the rest of the manual is detail.

The three layers

The mounts — where the work happens

Each mount is a self-contained four-axis robot: pan, tilt, slider and zoom. Two boards live on it — a Teensy 4.1 that drives the motors through silent TMC2209 stepper drivers, and an ESP32-S3 bridge with a small round touchscreen that carries the wireless link and shows live state. Crucially, the mount stores its own positions, subjects, calibration and settings in non-volatile memory — power it off and back on and it still knows everything.

The hub — the switchboard

A single ESP32-S3 hub runs the camera network. It creates its own WiFi access point (CamMount), holds an ESP-NOW wireless star out to the five mounts, and simultaneously runs a server for every kind of controller at once:

The wired hub There is a second hub build with an Ethernet port, for rigs that put the mounts on a wired network alongside everything else. It takes its wired address from DHCP and prints the address it was given at boot, next to its Ethernet MAC — so there is nothing to configure, provided the hub and satellites are registered on the network.

Its access point stays exactly as above: 192.168.4.1 is the hub's own WiFi, not its wired address, and phones and the PC app still reach it there whatever the wire is doing.

The controllers — interchangeable windows

The PC app, a phone, the console and a Stream Deck all speak to that same hub, all see the same state, and any of them can hand off to any other mid-show. There's no "master" controller. This works because the controllers are stateless — they don't own anything the rig needs to function.

Where settings live

This is the payoff of the model: almost everything the rig needs is stored on the mounts themselves. Only a few conveniences live in the PC app.

Why this matters Because the rig's memory lives on the mounts, a controller can crash, close, run out of battery or be swapped for a different one, and nothing is lost. Reconnect and every position and calibration is still there. The names are the one thing the PC app owns — see Camera & position names.

How a command flows

Say you tap recall shot 3 on camera 2 on your phone:

  1. The web app sends the request to the hub over WebSocket.
  2. The hub relays it over ESP-NOW to camera 2's bridge.
  3. The bridge hands it to the Teensy, which drives the motors to the stored position at the active speed preset.
  4. Status flows back the same way, so every connected surface updates — your phone, the PC app and the console all show camera 2 moving.
Safety runs underneath all of it Continuous jogging needs a live 20 Hz command stream. If any link in that chain goes quiet — hub, PC, WiFi — the mount stops itself within half a second. More in Troubleshooting.
