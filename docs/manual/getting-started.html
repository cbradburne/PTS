Getting started

From a box of powered-off parts to your first camera move.

This page assumes the hardware is built and the firmware is flashed. If you still need to build the firmware, see Building the firmware first.

What's in a system

A complete PTS rig is:

  • Up to five mounts — each a pan / tilt / slider / zoom camera head with a small round touchscreen.
  • One hub — a small box that creates its own WiFi network (CamMount) and links every mount to your controllers.
  • Optionally, a 7″ touchscreen console wired to the hub — enough to run the rig with no computer at all.
  • Your choice of controller — the PC app, a phone, a Stream Deck, or QLab. You can mix and match, and hand off between them mid-show.

1. Power on

  1. Power the hub first. After a few seconds it starts broadcasting its CamMount WiFi network.
  2. Power each mount. Its screen lights up showing the camera number and connection state.
  3. If you're using the 7″ console, power it too — it connects to the hub over a wired link automatically.
First time only A brand-new mount doesn't know which camera it is or which hub to talk to yet — its screen opens straight into Setup. That's the next step.

2. Pair each mount

Pairing tells a mount two things: which camera number it is (1–5) and which hub to use. It's done entirely on the mount's own touchscreen — no addresses to type, nothing to configure on the hub.

  1. On the mount, open Setup — a new mount opens it automatically; otherwise touch and hold the screen for about 1.5 seconds.
  2. Tap the camera number (1–5) that matches the label on the mount.
  3. The mount scans and lists the hubs it can see. Tap your hub (if only one is found it's pre-selected).
  4. Tap Save. The mount restarts and comes back paired — its ring turns green when it's talking to the hub.

The hub learns each mount the first time it hears from it, so there's nothing to do on the hub side. Repeat for each mount. Full detail, including what happens if two mounts pick the same number, is in Pairing & identity.

Moving between venues You can pair a mount to more than one hub (say, home and work). After that it finds whichever hub is powered on by itself — no re-pairing.

3. Connect a controller

Pick whichever suits you — they all talk to the same hub and show the same state.

The quickest: a phone

  1. On your phone, join the CamMount WiFi network.
  2. Open a browser to http://192.168.4.1. The web app loads — no install.

The full controller: the PC app

  1. Connect the PC to the hub — by USB, or over the CamMount WiFi.
  2. Launch the app, open Config, and set the connection (USB serial port, or TCP to 192.168.4.1).
  3. The five camera tiles come alive as the mounts report in.

See PC app and Web app for the full tour.

4. Home the axes

Before stored positions mean anything, each mount needs to know where its travel ends are. This is homing — the mount drives each axis gently to its end stop and zeroes it (no limit switches needed).

  1. In the PC app, open Config → the camera's tab → Homing.
  2. Home the slider and zoom as fitted. Pan and tilt are continuous and don't need homing.
Give it room Homing moves the axis to its physical limit. Make sure the mount is clear to move before you start.

5. Your first move

  1. Select a camera (tap its tile / number).
  2. Jog it with the joystick or the on-screen controls to frame a shot.
  3. Store the shot to a position slot (1–10).
  4. Move the camera somewhere else, then recall the slot — the mount travels back to the exact framing.

That's the core loop. From here:

  • Storing & recalling shots — speeds, clearing, the 10 slots.
  • Look-at tracking — keep a presenter framed while the camera slides.
  • Companion / QLab — put shots on Stream Deck buttons and show cues.
Safety Every mount stops itself within about half a second if it loses contact with its controller mid-move, and an E-STOP from any surface halts all axes at once. More in Troubleshooting.
