Pairing & identity

How a mount picks its camera number and hub — with no MAC addresses and no per-unit firmware.

Every board runs one identical binary. A mount isn't "camera 3" because of its firmware — it's camera 3 because someone tapped 3 on its screen. Identity and pairing are set entirely from touchscreens and stored in each mount's flash, so there's nothing to configure in source and no per-unit builds.

Pairing a mount

Pairing tells a mount two things: which camera number it is (1–5) and which hub to talk to. It's done on the mount's own round touchscreen.

  1. Open Setup — a brand-new mount opens it automatically; otherwise touch and hold the screen for about 1.5 seconds.
  2. Tap the camera number (1–5) that matches the mount's label.
  3. The mount scans and lists the hubs it can see. Tap your hub (if only one is found, it's pre-selected).
  4. Tap Save. The mount restarts and comes back paired — its ring turns green when it's talking to the hub.
The hub needs no setup The hub learns each mount the first time it hears from it — it binds on first contact. There's nothing to enter on the hub side. Repeat the steps above for each mount.

Renumbering

To change a mount's camera number, open Setup again and pick a different number. The hub picks up the change on the mount's next contact — renumbering "just works", no un-pairing step required.

When two mounts pick the same number

If two mounts claim the same camera number, the hub notices and raises a conflict prompt on the console — a one-tap Replace / Ignore choice — so you resolve it without digging through settings. Give one of them a different number in Setup and the conflict clears.

Moving between venues

Pair to more than one hub A mount can be paired to several hubs — say, one at home and one at work. After that it finds whichever hub is powered on by itself, with no re-pairing. Handy for a rig that travels.

Channel self-healing

WiFi environments change — a venue's channel plan won't match your bench. The hub and mounts negotiate and heal the ESP-NOW link automatically when the channel moves, so a rig set up in one place comes up working in another without intervention.

Forgetting a mount

The console's pairing UI can forget a mount the hub has learned — useful when retiring or swapping hardware, so a stale mount doesn't linger in the hub's list.

Under the hood Pairing rides on ESP-NOW, and identity lives in the mount's and hub's non-volatile storage — see How it fits together. The exact protocol is documented in the firmware sources for anyone building or modifying the system.
