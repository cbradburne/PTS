Pairing & identity

How a mount picks its camera number and hub — with no MAC addresses and no per-unit firmware.

Every board runs one identical binary. A mount isn't "camera 3" because of its firmware — it's camera 3 because someone tapped 3 on its screen. Identity and pairing are set entirely from touchscreens and stored in each mount's flash, so there's nothing to configure in source and no per-unit builds.

Pairing a mount

Pairing tells a mount two things: which camera number it is (1–5) and which hub to talk to. It's done on the mount's own round touchscreen.

  1. Open Setup — a brand-new mount opens it automatically; otherwise touch and hold the screen for about 1.5 seconds.
  2. Tap the camera number (1–5) that matches the mount's label.
  3. The mount scans and lists the hubs it can see. Tap your hub (if only one is found, it's pre-selected).
  4. Tap Save. The mount restarts and comes back paired — its ring turns green when it's talking to the hub.
The hub needs no setup The hub learns each mount the first time it hears from it — it binds on first contact. There's nothing to enter on the hub side. Repeat the steps above for each mount.

Pairing a camera

A mount can also pair over Bluetooth to a Blackmagic camera mounted on it, which is what lets the console drive iris, focus, zoom, gain and white balance. It's optional and per-mount — a rig can have one camera paired or five, and a mount with no camera never scans for one.

The mount goes off the air while pairing Bluetooth pairing needs the radio to itself, so the mount stops WiFi for the duration and cannot be driven from anything. It comes back on the air when you leave the screen — either by tapping BACK, or by leaving it alone for two minutes, after which it exits on its own. Pair before a show, not during one.
  1. Put the camera into pairing mode — on a Blackmagic body that's Setup → Bluetooth, and it will show a six-digit code.
  2. On the mount, touch and hold for about 1.5 seconds to open Setup, then hold again to reach CAMERA PAIRING. It's chained behind Setup deliberately: it's a once-per-mount commissioning job for whoever builds the rig, not something an operator needs to find.
  3. The mount scans, and the line under the title tells you where it is — Searching for a camera…, then Enter the code on the camera once it has found one. No camera in range means the scan came back empty; check the camera is actually advertising.
  4. Type the six-digit code on the keypad. Digits fill the dashes left to right, and ⌫ takes one back.
  5. Press the green tick. It only submits on a full six digits, so a short code does nothing rather than failing. The status goes green and reads PAIRED, or Pairing FAILED — try again, which usually means the code was mistyped or the camera stopped advertising while you typed.
Forgetting a camera FORGET clears the bond so the mount stops trying to reach that camera. Worth doing before a body moves to another mount — otherwise two mounts believe they own the same camera, and only one of them is right. A mount with no bond never scans, so forgetting also puts its Bluetooth back to fully idle.

Renumbering

To change a mount's camera number, open Setup again and pick a different number. The hub picks up the change on the mount's next contact — renumbering "just works", no un-pairing step required.

When two mounts pick the same number

If two mounts claim the same camera number, the hub notices and raises a conflict prompt on every controller at once — the hub touchscreen, the web app's Mounts page, and the PC app — a one-tap Replace / Ignore choice on any of them. Replace binds the new device to that camera; Ignore keeps the current one. Or just give one mount a different number in Setup and the conflict clears itself.

Moving between venues

Pair to more than one hub A mount can be paired to several hubs — say, one at home and one at work. After that it finds whichever hub is powered on by itself, with no re-pairing. Handy for a rig that travels.

Channel self-healing

WiFi environments change — a venue's channel plan won't match your bench. The hub and mounts negotiate and heal the ESP-NOW link automatically when the channel moves, so a rig set up in one place comes up working in another without intervention.

Managing pairing from any surface

The hub owns the pairing table; every controller is just a window onto it. The hub touchscreen, the web app's Mounts page, and the PC app's Config → Paired Mounts all show the same live table and can act on it — so a rig with just a hub and a phone (or a PC) is fully manageable, no console required.

Forgetting a mount

Any of those surfaces can forget a mount the hub has learned — useful when retiring or swapping hardware, so a stale mount doesn't linger in the hub's list. (A mount that's still powered on simply re-pairs itself within about five seconds, so Forget is for units that are gone.)

Under the hood Pairing rides on ESP-NOW, and identity lives in the mount's and hub's non-volatile storage — see How it fits together. The exact protocol is documented in the firmware sources for anyone building or modifying the system.
